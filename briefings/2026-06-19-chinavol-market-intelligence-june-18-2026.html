🗞️ Headline

The US-Iran ceasefire deal just detonated the oil market and triggered a macro regime shift — lower energy inflation + easing supply anxiety = China easing window is finally open.

---

Us Session Recap

The Fed held rates at 3.50%–3.75% on Wednesday but the dot plot did the heavy lifting: the 2026 median FF projection rose to 3.8%, erasing any cut expectation and pricing in a hike. New Chairman Kevin Warsh gutted the statement — down to 130 words from 341 — and stripped all easing-bias language. The market's reaction was swift: SPY sold off, the 10-year yield (^TNX: 4.44%) rose, and risk-off positioning accelerated. Meanwhile, WTI crude collapsed $2/barrel to $73.93 as the US-Iran MoU signed June 17 confirmed Hormuz reopening within 30 days, with Goldman Sachs projecting Gulf exports normalize by end-July. That's a direct disinflationary shock — exactly what the Fed claims to be "looking through," but which removes the main justification for staying hawkish.

---

China Watch

Two China-specific catalysts deserve attention today. First, the Lujiazui Forum in Shanghai delivered concrete policy signals: PBoC Governor Pan Gongsheng flagged further exploration of interest-rate mechanism reform toward a price-based system; CSRC Chairman Wu Qing pledged to accelerate "patient capital" inflows, noting social security and insurance funds have net-bought A-shares to the tune of ¥1.3 trillion over the past two years. Second, May macro data from the NBS showed industrial production up +4.5% YoY with high-tech manufacturing surging +15.1%, while PPI turned positive for the first time in 41 months at +2.8% YoY. Exports in yuan terms rose +13.8% YoY in May, driven by AI supply chain demand. The yuan fixed at 6.8130 versus USD Wednesday — firm, not under pressure — giving the PBoC room to ease asymmetrically in 2H. The EU is simultaneously hardening its China trade stance (€417B bilateral deficit, new defensive tools under discussion), but this is a medium-term risk, not an overnight shock. Watch: CSI 300 support levels and whether FXI ($33.26) and KWEB ($25.22) can hold the line on this macro tailwind.
