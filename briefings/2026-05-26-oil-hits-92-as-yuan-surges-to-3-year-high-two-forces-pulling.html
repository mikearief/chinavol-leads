### 今日要点 Oil's collapse and yuan's surge are a mixed bag for China: buy A-share tech on energy savings and FX gains, but trim export-heavy names before the PBOC signals it has had enough yuan appreciation.

Headline

Oil's shock drop below $91/bbl and the yuan's surge to its strongest level since 2023 are creating a split-screen dynamic for China — energy costs falling but export competitiveness at risk.

---

Us Session Recap

US equities hit fresh all-time highs Monday, with the Dow touching 50,500 as investors shrugged off hotter-than-expected inflation data (April CPI at 3.8%). The S&P 500 and Nasdaq extended gains, driven by AI/tech momentum. However, bond yields tell a different story: the 10-year Treasury climbed to 4.56%, its highest in weeks, pricing in a Fed on hold well into 2026. New Chair Kevin Warsh's first FOMC meeting is June 16–17, and the market has essentially zeroed out any rate-cut expectations for the year. VIX dropped to 16.59 — complacency is back.

---

China Watch

X traders are buzzing about a rare confluence: Brent crude plunging 6%+ in two days on US-Iran peace deal speculation, while the PBOC holds the yuan fixing firm near 6.83 — its strongest since 2023. Analysts on X are flagging this as a potential inflection point for A-shares: lower energy input costs benefit Chinese industrials, but a rapidly appreciating yuan threatens the export recovery Beijing desperately needs.

Semiconductor stocks are the standout trade: SMIC A-shares surged 10% on heavy volume as AI capex continues to drive demand. CSI 300 held 4,845 with tech names outperforming. However, the FX tailwind in USD terms is real — onshore A-share holders get a double bonus from both equity appreciation AND currency gains.

Watch the PBOC's next fixing closely. If the yuan breaks below 6.80 decisively, it signals Beijing is willing to sacrifice export competitiveness for currency prestige — a major signal for KWEB and FXI positioning.

---

Risk Flag

The November 2026 trade truce expiration looms large. With only ~$30 billion in tariff rollback announced and the US indicating it is "not in a rush," the tactical ceasefire could snap back into a full trade war — especially if Iran tensions ease and oil falls further, removing a key inflation hedge.
