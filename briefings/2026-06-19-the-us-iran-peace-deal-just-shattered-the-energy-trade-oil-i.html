Us Session Recap

Wall Street closed higher Friday after Trump and Iran signed a framework MOU to end the US-Israel conflict and reopen the Strait of Hormuz — a deal that sent Brent crude crashing nearly 40% from its conflict peak to $78.24. The disinflationary shock overrode the Fed's hawkish stance: rates held at 3.5-3.75%, the dot plot erased any 2026 cut, and headline PCE inflation was revised up to 3.6%. Markets shrugged — lower oil is the bigger story. SPY at 746.74, QQQ at 740.62, with tech outperforming on rate-cut replacement hopes.

China Watch

China is caught between a gift and a threat. The oil collapse is a direct win: lower import costs, reduced manufacturing energy burdens, and a stronger yuan as risk-off fades. The CSI 300 has gained 10% YTD and is showing lower implied volatility than the VIX — a structural shift away from the boom-bust past. Morgan Stanley data shows $1.3 billion in foreign inflows via northbound links in April alone. But the tariff sword remains: Trump's Beijing visit this week with Boeing, Qualcomm, and Citigroup executives signals hard pressure for purchase commitments. US tariffs on China still average 34% (Congressional Research Service, Feb 2026), and the October 2025 truce is fragile. Watch for: (1) whether Trump extracts binding purchase commitments, (2) yuan strength continuation as energy-driven risk-off fades, (3) whether CSI 300 holds above 3,800 on renewed foreign interest.

Risk Flag

The 60-day Iran truce could unravel at any moment — shipping firms are already cautious about Hormuz durability, and a single incident restarts the oil spike that destabilizes everything China-focused.

Trade Signal

Buy KWEB on any dip below $25. The Iran peace removes a geopolitical discount suppressing China equity risk premia. With $32 trillion in domestic deposits maturing in 2026 and flowing into financial assets, the structural bid is real — and a de-escalating US-Iran conflict makes Beijing's negotiating position less desperate.
