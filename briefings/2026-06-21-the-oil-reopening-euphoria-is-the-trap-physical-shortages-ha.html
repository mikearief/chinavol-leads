The Oil Reopening Euphoria Is The Trap — Physical Shortages Haven't Been Priced

Us Session Recap

Warsh's first FOMC held rates at 3.50-3.75% but the dot plot now points to 3.8% by year-end — at least one hike coming. The new chairman gutted forward guidance, slashed the statement to 130 words (from 341 in April), and launched five monetary-policy task forces. Markets are pricing an October hike. SPY at 746.74, QQQ at 740.62 — both range-bound as the hawkish pivot sinks in. VIX at 16.78, low but not pricing the oil-storage risk.

China Watch

The US-China trade truce is holding after Trump's May Beijing visit, but tariffs still average ~48% on Chinese goods. Beijing signaled it will accept a negotiated rate near 30% and wants to extend the November deadline. A-shares are in a new cyclical uptrend: Goldman raised its CSI 300 12-month target to 5,500, Guotai Haitong sees Q3 index highs. The AI supply chain and export engine are driving a 20% EPS growth forecast. The yuan is firming below 6.80, giving the PBoC cover to stay patient. FXI at 33.30, KWEB at 25.24 — both cheap relative to the rally in onshore names.

Risk Flag

The market is celebrating the Strait of Hormuz reopening as if the oil deficit is a solved problem. It isn't. 1.15 billion barrels of supply were lost during the closure. US strategic reserves are at a 43-year low. Cushing storage hit operational stress levels. Trump himself warned reserves could run out in ~4 weeks. Traders are pricing a glut. The opposite may be true.

Trade Signal

BUY crude oil here. CL=F at $76.54 is pricing a perfect supply resumption that ignores de-mining timelines, months-long inventory rebuild, and the ~2.4 mb/d global deficit the Oxford Institute projects for Q2. The OIES forecasts Brent averaging $108/b this quarter and $92 for the full year. If the physical shortage bites by August — and it will — crude re-rates to $95+. This is the contrarian trade of the quarter: sell the complacency, buy the barrel.
