VIX dropped 5.50% to 15.13, placing implied volatility in a calm regime and supporting tactical exposure to liquid China beta. That calm faces two immediate tests: this week's PCE inflation release and Chair Kevin Warsh's Jackson Hole address on Friday, where a hold-or-hike bias would pressure long-duration technology and ADR multiples. Low VIX alongside $90-plus Brent argues for disciplined upside participation rather than complacent leverage.

今日要闻 (Today's Headlines)

  • Washington is widening enforcement against Chinese overcapacity and third-country transshipment. Active Section 301 scrutiny across autos, batteries, semiconductors, solar, steel and machinery keeps export-heavy A-shares and supply-chain equities exposed to headline shocks.
  • US-Canada trade talks broke down over steel-origin and China-transshipment rules. The dispute shows that Washington's China tariff perimeter is now affecting allies, raising compliance costs across North American supply chains used by Chinese exporters.
  • US inflation cooled at the headline level but services remain sticky. The market has largely eliminated 2026 rate cuts, keeping the dollar-liquidity backdrop restrictive for the yuan, Hong Kong growth stocks and leveraged China beta.
  • China's weak July data have shifted attention from fundamentals to policy delivery. Fiscal support, PBOC liquidity operations and September stimulus expectations are carrying A-share sentiment while property, consumption and deflation signals remain weak.
  • China is using import cuts and inventories to absorb the Middle East oil shock. Lower crude prices reduce China's import bill today, but eventual stockpile rebuilding preserves upside risk for oil and downstream input costs.

地缘风险与宏观瞭望 (Geopolitical Risk & Macro Outlook)

The automated risk monitor registered 0/100 across Taiwan/China, the Middle East, Ukraine/Eastern Europe and US macro, with no multi-source convergence alert; the Polymarket scan also found no active Taiwan/China sentinel event among 200 markets. The macro regime is restrictive but not recessionary: fed funds stand at 3.63%, the 10Y-2Y curve is positively sloped at +0.50 percentage point, unemployment is 4.1%, and the July CPI index rose to 332.813 from 332.568. China's immediate risk is therefore policy and trade rather than acute geopolitics—domestic growth is weak, tariff enforcement is broadening, and the Fed offers little valuation relief. Favor selective domestic-policy beneficiaries over export-sensitive cyclicals, keep China internet exposure tactical after BABA's selloff, and use Friday's Jackson Hole message as the key duration-risk checkpoint.

Trade the stimulus window selectively: prefer domestic-policy beneficiaries and oil consumers, while treating export exposure and long-duration ADRs as headline-sensitive positions.
